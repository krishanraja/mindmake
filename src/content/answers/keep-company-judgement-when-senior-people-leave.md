When a senior person leaves, the handover usually goes well on paper. Their tasks are listed, their contacts are introduced and their files are moved. Six months later the business makes a decision they would have stopped, and nobody can say why it feels wrong until it has already gone wrong.

What left was their judgement: how they decided, not what they did.

## Why handover documents miss it

Handover documents answer the question "what does this person do?" They are written at the end, often in a hurry, by someone who is already thinking about their next role. They list responsibilities, open work and useful contacts.

They rarely answer "how does this person decide?" That knowledge is hard to write on demand. It lives in reactions: the pricing request they always refused, the supplier they never trusted, the kind of hire that looked good and failed. Asked directly, most experts struggle to list it. Shown a real decision, they know immediately.

## Record decisions as they happen

The practical fix is to record decisions as they happen, with their reasons, while the person is still making them. Each entry needs four things: the situation, what was decided, why, and what would have changed the answer.

Over a few months, patterns appear. The same standard shows up in five different decisions. An exception gets made twice for the same reason. That is judgement becoming visible, and it can now be taught.

## Make it usable by people and AI

A record like this is useful to a new hire, who can read how similar calls were made before. It is also useful to an AI system, which can draw on the record when drafting a recommendation and name the past decision it relied on.

That second use matters more each year. Teams increasingly start their work with AI. If the AI starts from the public average, the business gets average work. If it starts from the business's own record of judgement, the departed expert's reasoning is still part of every draft.

## Keep it owned by the business

The record should sit in the business's own accounts, readable by people and exportable. If it lives inside a vendor's system, the business ends up renting back its own memory.

This shared record is different from a leader's own AI brain, which is private and stays theirs wherever they go. What the business keeps is what they chose to hand to the team.

Start with one person and one recurring decision. Record the next five times it is made and the reasons behind each one. That small record is worth more at their leaving party than any handover document.
