# Value Proposition

---

## Core value proposition

**"Mindmaker is the anti-consultancy for leaders done being sold AI. Two engagements. The Teardown takes one real decision apart in ten business days. The Handover spends six weeks rebuilding how the business decides and sells, and then ends. A capped practice: a small number of engagements a year."**

The enemy statement, which is the sharpest sentence on the site and does not change:

> Consultants, LLMs and the next hyped tool sell you point solutions built to extract your judgment, not build it.

One ICP: companies of 50 to 5,000 people, sweet spot 100 to 1,000, where the buyer is the CEO, CRO or VP Product. Funds buying for a portfolio company are a third door into the same two engagements, not a separate audience.

---

## Key differentiators

### 1. Two rungs, not a ladder of upsells
**Them:** Discovery, then a workshop, then a strategy phase, then implementation, then a retainer. Every step an upsell.
**Us:** Two engagements. Both fixed-scope, both with a date they end. The smaller one is the gate for the larger one, and it regularly says no.
**Why:** Middle-tier consulting is where scope creeps, outcomes blur and fees compound. It is not sold here.

### 2. Operator, not advisor
**Them:** Strategists who researched AI.
**Us:** Krish runs a 14-agent AI operating system across his own portfolio every day, 45 workflows in production, licensed to three businesses. Sixteen years commercialising content, media and IP businesses before that.
**Why:** The commercial work comes out of production rather than out of a deck. See `/operator`.

### 3. The disagreement is the product
**Them:** One confident answer, delivered with certainty proportional to the invoice.
**Us:** Four models cross-examine the decision and where they disagree, you see the disagreement.
**Why:** Averaging four models into one confident answer hides exactly the part you needed to look at. Certainty is the easiest thing to fake and the most expensive thing to buy wrongly.

### 4. Non-dependency, made falsifiable
**Them:** "Strategic partnership." Open-ended engagements and monthly invoices.
**Us:** In week five of the Handover, Krish does not attend at all. A Day 90 recheck is included.
**Why:** Everyone claims their work makes you independent. Week five is where that stops being a claim and becomes something the client watches happen, while there is still time to fix it if it does not hold.

### 5. Published prices
**Them:** "Let's get you on a call to discuss investment levels."
**Us:** Both figures are on the website, in three currencies, and the agent on the site quotes them exactly.
**Why:** A buyer who has to book a call to learn a price has been told something about the relationship before it starts.

### 6. A capped practice
**Them:** Capacity scales with headcount, so the person you bought is not the person you get.
**Us:** Six Handovers a year, across every client. Krish does the work.
**Why:** It is the honest number for work done personally, and saying so is more useful than pretending otherwise. It is also why the cap is stated publicly rather than used as a closing device.

## Positioning by buyer

### The CEO, CRO or VP Product

**Their situation:** The product works. The commercial story does not. The deck has been rewritten four times and nobody has said out loud that the problem is that no one can explain what the company sells, to whom, at what price.

**What they have tried:** Fourteen vendor conversations. An internal working group. A strategy offsite. Possibly a consultancy that left a deck and took the context with them.

**What they want:** To stop relitigating the same decision, and to be able to defend the call they make.

**The answer:** A Teardown if there is one decision. A Handover if the decision is made and the machine around it is what is broken.

### The operating partner or fund

**Their situation:** They already know which portfolio company is stuck, and they suspect the same problem is showing up at more than one of them.

**What they want:** A read they can trust that is not the founder's own account, and something that leaves the company able to run without an ongoing dependency they now own.

**The answer:** The same two engagements, priced per portfolio company. Fund-level terms on the call.

## Product positioning

### The Teardown
**One line:** Bring the decision you keep not making.
**Position:** The entry rung and the gate. Ten business days, under two hours of the client's time.
**Against:** A research report, a strategy deck, a discovery phase.
**Proof it is honest:** It has talked people out of the Handover as often as into it.

### The Handover
**One line:** Six weeks. Then I leave and you keep it.
**Position:** The rebuild. Six weeks on how the business decides and sells, plus a Day 90 recheck.
**Against:** A six-month engagement, a fractional executive, a retainer.
**Proof it is honest:** Week five, where Krish does not attend.

### CTRL
**Position:** A separate product with its own site and its own pricing. Not sold here. It appears as a Teardown deliverable and as a link.

## What Mindmaker does not sell

No fractional executive roles. No ongoing retainers. No production IT or implementation work. No training. Nothing without a finish line.

**And no discounts.** There is no published credit, no percentage off, no urgency offer. A published discount trains every buyer to wait for it.
