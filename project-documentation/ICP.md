**Last Updated:** 2026-05-15

## Three Audiences, No Overlap

Mindmaker is a ladder with a third inquiry-only doorway. There are three primary ICPs and a retention layer (the Alumni Pass). The old "Builder vs Orchestrator" split was a 1:1 sprint framing and has been retired along with the public 1:1 sprint product.

| ICP | Primary entry | Primary depth offer | Doorway |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI Leaders** (senior operators making AI decisions) | A Mindmaker Workshop ($599) for technical-curious leaders ready to build; The AI-Fluent Executive Cohort ($2,500) for leaders sitting on a nervous decision | The AI-Fluent Executive (Cohort) | `/workshops`, `/cohort` (Maven enrolment) |
| **AI Products** (companies commercializing AI capability) | The Signal Session ($15,000) | Signal Session → Revenue Architecture ($60–100k) | `/enterprise` |
| **Executive Teams** (CEO-sponsored leadership groups) | The AI Immersion (inquiry-only, $12,000) | The AI Immersion | `/immersion` |
| **Alumni** (retention layer, not a primary acquisition path) | The Alumni Pass ($1,500/year) | The Alumni Pass | `/alumni` (invitation-only, unlinked) |

## ICP 1: AI Leaders: Cohort Buyer

**Who:** Senior operators with budget authority and a nervous AI decision. Not looking to learn AI in the abstract, looking to make one specific call and feel good about it.

**Typical titles:** CEO, COO, CFO, CPO, CCO, CMO, GM, VP of Product, VP of Strategy, Chief of Staff to a C-suite owner, founder-operator running a P&L.

**Experience:** 10+ years in leadership roles. P&L or budget authority. Delegates execution but owns outcomes.

### Firmographic context

| Attribute | Range |
|---|---|
| Stage | Series B to public, or mid-market privately held |
| Revenue | $10M–$500M (sweet spot $25M–$250M) |
| Employees | 50–2,000 |
| Geography | Primarily US, UK, EU, ANZ. English-first cohorts |
| Budget authority | $500k+ for the relevant decision |

### Psychographic signals

- Has been pitched 14+ AI tools this quarter and committed to none
- Says some version of "I should understand this but I don't"
- Presents AI strategies to boards they can't actually execute
- Watches AI-native competitors moving faster and feels the cost of catching up compounding
- Trusts peers more than vendors

### Workshop-buyer signal (sub-segment of AI Leaders)

A subset of AI Leaders are technical-curious and ready to build something this quarter. They don't yet have a singular nervous decision; they want to *do* in order to *understand*. Workshops at $599 are the right entry rung. Indicators:

- "I've watched AI from the sidelines long enough; I want to ship something with it"
- "I want a chief of staff that actually shows up to work"
- "I'm tired of waiting for IT, I want to build the internal tool myself"
- "Our agent strategy is theoretical, I want to map it for real"
- "We've tried five tools; I want to give my AI memory that survives Monday"

A successful Workshop attendee often graduates to the Cohort (the $500 `WORKSHOP` discount code at Maven checkout is the ramp).

### Nervous decisions we hear every week

- "Do we build or buy our AI stack?"
- "Which vendors do we commit to, and which do we cut?"
- "How do I tell the board what our AI strategy actually is?"
- "What do I replace vs what do I empower?"
- "Can I trust ChatGPT Enterprise / Copilot / Gemini claims?"
- "How do we set AI governance without slowing everything down?"
- "My team is using 14 different tools. It's chaos."

### How they talk about the problem

- "I've been pitched 14 tools this quarter. I use none of them."
- "I should understand this but I don't."
- "I'm making it up in board meetings and it's starting to show."
- "My peers are either way ahead or way behind. I can't tell which."

### What they want from the cohort

- A board-ready position on one nervous decision
- Peer validation from 10–15 others working the same problem
- A framework for the next decision, not dependence on us
- Zero vendor pitch. Zero deck theatre.

### What they become after the cohort

A senior leader with one defensible AI decision documented (1-page memo), a peer network of 10–15 operators, 90 days of cohort Slack access, lifetime curriculum access, alumni network membership, and a portable framework for the next nervous decision.

### Buying triggers (signals to watch for in outbound)

- New CEO / new board / fresh fundraise → board AI narrative pressure
- AI-native competitor announcement in their category
- Failed vendor pilot ("we spent six months on Copilot and have no answer")
- Public statement from CEO that AI is a top-3 priority with no clear next step
- Hire of a "Head of AI" without a Chief of Strategy partner

### Disqualifiers

- Wants 1:1 attention (route to inquiry-only path or disqualify)
- Pre-revenue or pre-product-market fit
- Looking for a course or certification
- Wants implementation or production IT
- Cannot name a specific decision in a sentence

## ICP 2: AI Products: Enterprise Buyer

**Who:** Founders, CEOs, or C-suite operators at companies that have built an AI product or AI-enabled capability and can't translate it into revenue predictably. The tech exists. The pricing, positioning, and GTM were built for a non-AI product, or built fast without a commercial lens.

**Typical titles:** Founder / CEO, President, Chief Commercial Officer, Chief Revenue Officer, Chief Product Officer, VP of GTM, Head of Commercial Strategy.

**Experience:** Scaling beyond founder-led sales, or post-Series B, or public company retrofitting GTM around a new AI product line.

### Firmographic context

| Attribute | Range |
|---|---|
| Stage | Series B+, late-stage private, or public |
| Revenue | $10M–$1B+ (Signal Session at the lower end, Revenue Architecture at the higher end) |
| AI context | Has shipped AI product or AI-enabled capability in last 12 months |
| Symptom | Deal sizes erratic; sales can't articulate differentiation; pricing is guesswork; GTM doesn't match buyer motion |

### Psychographic signals

- Tech is real but revenue isn't scaling predictably
- "Everyone says AI-powered", they're losing the differentiation game
- Investor pressure for the "AI → revenue" story they can't tell confidently
- Sales team pushing back on positioning ("I can't sell this against [competitor]")
- Pricing leadership in flux, discounting heavily to close deals

### Commercial problems we hear every week

- "We have the tech. We don't have the pitch."
- "Pricing is guesswork. What's this actually worth?"
- "Our GTM was built for a non-AI product. It doesn't fit."
- "Sales can't articulate why we're different from the 14 other AI tools in our category."
- "Deal sizes are all over the place. We can't forecast."
- "We're moving upmarket but our ICP is shifting and nobody agrees on who it is."

### What they want

- A commercial diagnosis from someone who's operated, not just advised
- Positioning, pricing, packaging, GTM rebuilt around the AI capability
- A 90-day GTM playbook with sales process and enablement
- A board-ready narrative that translates AI investment into revenue
- A 30-day timeline, not a 6-month engagement

### What they become

A company with a clear commercial thesis for its AI product, pricing that reflects value, sales that can articulate differentiation, packaging that maps to the buyer journey, and a roadmap the board can back.

### Buying triggers (signals to watch for in outbound)

- New product launch or AI feature ship in the last 6 months
- Recent hire of a CRO, CCO, or VP GTM
- Fresh Series B/C/D round with "go-to-market scaling" in the press release
- Public commentary from CEO that "we need to nail the commercial story"
- Pricing page changes, sudden tier consolidations, or "request a quote" replacing self-serve
- Public comparison content from competitors that the company can't match

### Disqualifiers

- Pre-revenue / pre-product
- Wants implementation, deployment, or fractional executive support
- Looking for a 6-month engagement (we don't sell that)
- Expects associates / a delivery team
- Can't move on the output within 90 days post-engagement

## ICP 3: Executive Teams: Immersion Buyer (inquiry-only)

**Who:** A CEO sponsoring a 4–8 person leadership group that needs rapid alignment on shared AI tensions. Not an individual decision; not a commercial rebuild; a team conversation that needs structure.

**Typical sponsors:** CEO, founder-CEO, COO, President. The sponsor pays; the team participates.

### Firmographic context

| Attribute | Range |
|---|---|
| Company stage | Series B+ or established mid-market |
| Team composition | 4–8 senior leaders (C-suite + key VPs) |
| Geography | Anywhere; on-site preferred when budget allows |
| Symptom | Team has had the same three AI conversations four times without resolution |

### Psychographic signals

- Sponsoring CEO can name three specific tensions in a sentence
- Team meetings circle the same questions without producing decisions
- Leaders are talking past each other, no shared vocabulary
- "We need a strategy day but actually facilitated, not a vendor pitch"

### Tensions we resolve in the room

- Vendor commitments (which platform do we standardize on?)
- Build vs buy at the team level (which capabilities do we own vs rent?)
- Replace vs empower (which roles change shape, on what timeline?)
- Board narrative (what's the team's shared AI story?)
- Governance and policy (what do we ship next quarter?)

### What they want

- A facilitated half-day where the team actually decides
- A neutral operator running the room (not a vendor, not a coach)
- A 2-page summary that's board-ready and doesn't need redaction
- Three named owners and three named deadlines

### What they become

A team genuinely aligned (not performatively aligned) on the three issues addressed, with named owners, named deadlines, and a board-ready 2-pager.

### Disqualifiers

- More than 8 people (format breaks past 8)
- Wants a multi-session engagement (we don't run multi-session Immersions)
- Wants the session recorded (recording kills candor)
- Sponsor cannot name three specific tensions to resolve
- Substitute participants expected (the format depends on the actual leaders being in the room)

## Anti-ICPs (who we explicitly won't serve)

- **Technical teams without commercial authority**. engineers, data scientists, ML platform leads. They need code or compute, not a decision room.
- **Early-stage founders with no revenue**. ship product-market fit first; our offers assume you have revenue to reshape.
- **Individual contributors or aspiring leaders**. we serve decision-makers with budget.
- **Training buyers**. AI literacy courses, certifications, learning-and-development programmes. Not our shape.
- **Fractional-role seekers**. fractional CAIO, CTO, CMO, CRO arrangements. We don't do ongoing capacity.
- **Retainer buyers**. month-to-month advisory. Every Mindmaker offer has a finish line.
- **Vendor co-sellers**. companies looking for a partner to recommend their tool. We're tool-agnostic.
- **Production IT buyers**. deployment, integration, managed services. Explicitly out of scope.

## Qualification Signals (use in PreCallQualifier and intake calls)

### Strong fit: Cohort

- Has one specific AI decision they can name in a sentence
- Board, peers, or investors are asking about AI
- Has been pitched by multiple vendors and feels no closer to a decision
- Says some version of "I should understand this but I don't"
- 10–15 peers at similar seniority would be useful, not threatening
- Comfortable with Maven as the platform for cohort enrolment and community

### Strong fit: Signal Session

- Has AI product in market, or launching within 90 days
- Deal sizes or pricing feel wrong, but the team hasn't pinned down why
- Commercial team (sales, marketing, pricing) is arguing internally about GTM
- Revenue is real but traction is uneven, "it works for some customers, not consistently"
- Budget owner willing to spend $15k to get a 15–20 page Commercial Narrative + 30-day roadmap

### Strong fit: Revenue Architecture

- Signal Session identified a structural commercial gap worth fixing OR they walk in already knowing the gap
- Company has $10M+ revenue and AI capability that is meaningfully differentiated
- Leadership agrees the GTM / positioning / pricing needs a rebuild, not a tweak
- Timeline allows 30-day engagement and ability to act on output within 90 days
- Budget authority for $60–100k with 50/50 payment terms

### Strong fit: Immersion

- CEO sponsorship + 4–8 senior leaders
- Three specific tensions named in a sentence
- Real candor allowed in the room (no recording, no observer politics)
- Outcome the sponsor wants: named decisions, named owners, named deadlines
- Travel budget if on-site is preferred

### Not a fit (regardless of offer)

- "Just exploring AI" with no nervous decision or commercial symptom
- Wants ongoing retainer or fractional support
- Wants implementation, deployment, or production IT
- Wants tool recommendations without trade-off analysis
- Expects us to run the decision or the strategy for them ongoing

## Entry Points

Every public path lands at **"Book a call"** via the global `ScopingModal` (`InitialConsultModal` is legacy, retained only for `/alumni`). The `PreCallQualifier` floating pill helps self-classify before the call so the 30-minute window is spent on the actual decision. The homepage hero also exposes a tertiary "Or start with a free lesson →" link to the Maven instructor page for cold buyers not yet ready to talk.

- Workshop-curious leaders land at `/workshops`, browse the five sub-pages, and enrol on Maven (or get notified for unpublished workshops). Workshop alumni get $500 off the Cohort with code `WORKSHOP`.
- Cohort-curious leaders land at `/cohort` and can either book a call or click straight through to Maven enrolment.
- Enterprise-curious buyers land at `/enterprise`.
- Warm inbound (blog, podcast, LinkedIn, referrals) lands on `/` and forks at the homepage intent fork (Sharpen / Resolve / Rebuild), routing to `/cohort`, `/enterprise#signal-session`, and `/capital` respectively.
- Immersion inquiries flow through `Book a call` → modal preselects "immersion".
- 1:1 inquiry-only path lives at `/cohort?inquiry=1:1` (not advertised on the main page).
- Alumni invitations: Krish sends invited alumni a direct link to `/alumni`. The page is `noindex` and unlinked from nav and footer.
