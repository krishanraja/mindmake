A media business owned by private equity or venture investors runs on a clock. Its owners plan to hold it for a period and sell it for more, so every big project is asked the same question: will this make the company worth more while we own it?

AI makes that question harder, because there are so many places to use it. Newsrooms can draft faster, sales teams can research faster, and every department can produce a list of uses. The list is rarely the problem. The order is.

## Why the list of uses is the wrong place to start

A list of AI uses by department spreads effort thinly. Each team saves some time, nobody can say what the time was spent on, and the board sees cost without a story. A year later the business has more tools and the same strategy.

The businesses that get value start from the other end. They ask which decision, if it were made better, would change what the company is worth. Then they build AI around that one decision first.

## The four levers

For most media businesses that decision sits on one of four levers:

- **Product.** What you make and for whom, now that AI can make a passable version of much of it.
- **Price.** What buyers will pay for, when the cost of making content keeps falling.
- **Positioning.** Why an advertiser, a subscriber or a partner should choose you over a cheaper machine-made option.
- **People.** Which work your team should keep, and which work AI should carry.

A media business has assets AI cannot copy: relationships with an audience, rights to an archive, a brand people trust and editors with judgement. The strongest first projects make one of those assets worth more.

## Build one working version and prove it

Pick the decision. Build a working first version of the system that supports it, using the tools you already have where you can. Use it on real work, with the leaders who own the result, and judge it by whether they would stand behind it.

That gives the board something to test rather than a plan to trust, and it gives the leadership team a capability they keep.
