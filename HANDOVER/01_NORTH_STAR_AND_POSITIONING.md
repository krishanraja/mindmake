# North Star, positioning and Krish's judgement

This document preserves the big picture extracted through the multi-week repositioning process. It is deliberately more complete than homepage copy. The public site should reveal this progressively, not try to fit it all in one headline.

## The North Star

Mindmake helps leaders put their best judgement to work with AI.

It takes care of the expertise, taste, standards, memory and context that made a leader valuable in the first place, then uses AI to amplify those things. The end goal is not more automation. The end goal is a leader who makes better decisions, directs AI with a working understanding and creates more business value.

The strongest work connects two levels:

1. The person becomes more capable, confident and current.
2. The business makes a materially better commercial move.

That commercial move may concern product strategy, company strategy, pricing, positioning, route to market, sales, content, data, intellectual property or the company structure that supports them.

## The public promise

The simplest hinge is:

**You can hand over the work. Not the understanding.**

Leaders can use consultants, language models, point tools and all-in-one systems. Mindmake is not hostile to those choices. The risk is that the work moves while the leader's own understanding stands still. If the learning remains with other people and tools, the leader becomes less able to test the work, ask the next question and make the call.

Mindmake works beside the leader so they can direct the help, keep their judgement current and retain the capability after the engagement.

## The two public doors

The site uses two doors because they meet non-technical buyers where they already are.

### Build Your AI Brain

Encode how a leader judges, remembers, chooses and creates. Help them:

- use what they know without carrying it all in their head;
- future-proof themselves as a leader;
- amplify their vision across the business;
- get better at things they avoid or are weak at;
- understand blind spots;
- make a better decision faster and with more confidence;
- buy back time and reinvest it in work with a higher value;
- build a personal AI chief of staff without giving away their human spark.

### Build Your AI GTM

Define how the business creates and captures value as AI changes the market. Help it decide:

- what customers can now do for themselves;
- what they will still pay for;
- what the product should become;
- how the offer, price and message should change;
- how sales should work with new data and earlier signals;
- how content, data and intellectual property can be commercialised;
- what the team should own;
- how a strong AI-native or AI-enabled company can grow, not merely avoid decline.

These are not unrelated products. Either can be the hook, and the paid need may sit behind the other door. Qualification must discover which idea attracts the visitor and which problem creates value.

The two doors and the central message move apart together. That movement is the meaning: one shared foundation opens into two useful starting routes. Do not turn it back into two static offer cards or place abstract visual noise behind them.

## The value ladder

AI can create three levels of value:

1. **Help me do the task.** Drafting, admin, analysis and faster execution.
2. **Bring me what matters.** Useful information arrives before the leader has to seek it.
3. **Extend what I can do.** Memory, judgement and systems compound into a new capability.

Mindmake is built for level three. Levels one and two support the work but are not the premium promise.

Time saved is only the start. The strategic question is whose time comes back, what they reinvest it in and whether the new capability keeps compounding.

## Best-fit buyers

The strongest buyer is a founder, principal, portfolio owner, investor or senior commercial leader who can move a decision and has enough influence for the result to matter.

Strong situations include:

- a well-connected principal with influence across several ventures who wants an executive AI chief of staff;
- a founder whose time is consumed by work that should no longer require them;
- a portfolio owner who wants more high-value time for companies, relationships and decisions;
- a healthy or fast-growing AI company whose product, price, positioning or company design needs to keep up with its market;
- an established internet, software, media, data, content, coaching, consulting or advisory business whose commercial rules are changing;
- a leader who is curious enough to build a working understanding and committed enough to use what is built.

Do not write only for anxious companies in decline. Krish wants to help ambitious, fast-moving businesses as well as established businesses under pressure. The tone should hold urgency and opportunity together.

## Commercial model

The paid starting point is a tightly bounded 30-day proof. There is no public price.

The proof chooses one important decision or capability, builds a working first version, uses it on real work and leaves behind something the client can keep using. It is not a month of discovery followed by slides or a folder of Markdown files.

The proof succeeds when the client says the working system is useful in practice. A good voluntary testimonial or case study is strong supporting evidence, subject to consent.

The best work continues for three months or longer. Continuation is retained revenue, but it earns its place by finding and building the next valuable opportunity. It must not create dependency.

Private commercial constraints:

- no more than two new proofs run at once;
- each proof should use no more than 24 to 30 hours of Krish's time;
- a third paying client should already be in a lighter continuation phase;
- the work should be worth well above $300 per hour once cash and compounding value are considered;
- the standalone first month costs more than one third of a three-month relationship;
- if the client continues, month one is recognised at the lower multi-month unit rate;
- terms may flex for rare equity, participation, access, relationships, content or case-study value, but exposure is not payment.

Do not publish these private economics without new approval.

## What Krish wants to compound

Hands-on building is valuable when it also strengthens something Mindmake can keep:

- reusable methods and delivery systems;
- anonymised benchmarks, trends, shifts and comparisons between moments in time;
- relationships and access;
- content and proof;
- owned intelligence that makes the next engagement sharper;
- selective equity or participation in exceptional opportunities.

Commodity agent libraries are not the moat. The stronger long-term content and intelligence edge is longitudinal: how AI use, value and monetisation change over time.

Client files, private data and client-specific outputs remain protected. Only reusable methods and properly anonymised, consented patterns can compound for Mindmake.

## Proof of practice

CTRL is Mindmake's product and evidence layer. It proves that Krish builds and uses the systems he recommends. It keeps memory, sources, checks and the human decision visible. CTRL is not a third public offer and not an automated replacement for Krish.

Examples of the broader practice include:

- implementing coding cultures inside commercial teams;
- testing AI pricing models;
- building AI-assisted content and sales engines;
- bringing useful sales data to teams earlier in the day;
- presenting editorial teams with useful options;
- building memory structures and sophisticated harnesses;
- building a personal contact system that can summon the right person from a large relationship graph;
- using agentic operating systems in Krish's own ventures.

Public proof must protect private contacts, client data, raw records and unverified claims.

## Conversion philosophy

There is no public diary link. A direct calendar makes the work feel lower value and lets unqualified visitors set the next step.

The main action is `Start here`.

The journey must give value before asking for contact details:

1. The visitor gives a company website.
2. Mindmake shows what it can establish and offers a small set of likely pressures.
3. The visitor makes one easy choice.
4. Mindmake shows what AI can carry, what should remain human and what a 30-day proof could test.
5. The visitor may give an email to keep the designed private brief.
6. The visitor gets the useful brief. Krish gets the same inputs plus company, fit and route context.
7. Krish decides whether a private conversation is useful.

The user should feel that every step pays them back. Do not preach, manufacture suspense or hide qualification behind questions that provide no visitor value.

The preferred second question is: `If you got more of your best time back, where would you put it?`

The publication choice is separate, optional and unticked. There is no automatic nurture sequence in the current build.

## Content and publication

The publication is the mass-distribution and credibility layer. It should use one subscriber relationship, not create a second disconnected marketing system.

The two editorial pillars are:

- `Building with AI`
- `The Money of AI`

The intended publication domain is `content.mindmake.co`. Until the move is verified, the current first-party publication destination remains in use.

## What the visitor should feel

Two user phrases are useful emotional acceptance criteria:

- `I'm feeling like I can navigate what's next and future proof myself.`
- `I'm building my AI brain and becoming an AI-native leader.`

The site should feel at the bleeding edge while being devastatingly simple to use. An experienced visitor should feel helped, not lectured. The intelligence should become more apparent as interest deepens, without show-off technology theatre.

## Krish's voice

Krish is conversational, direct, commercially literate and reassuring. He explains hard things without making the reader feel stupid. He is comfortable naming a sharp risk but does not shame beginners or pretend he has every answer.

Public writing rules:

- British English;
- short sentences and one idea at a time;
- understandable to a ten-year-old;
- no `thesis`;
- no em dashes;
- no generic consultant filler;
- no arrogant AI tone;
- no doom-only positioning;
- approved quotes stay verbatim;
- copy must explain value, a trade-off or evidence, not merely persuade.

## Krish's design taste

The target is distinctive editorial confidence, real-world warmth and interaction that makes the idea easier to understand.

Krish responds positively to:

- the clarity and design discipline of Tenex;
- the expressive starts and interaction quality of high-end studios such as Wavespace and Pear;
- visual systems where movement changes the meaning;
- editorial composition with strong rhythm, not generic stacked consultancy blocks;
- real footage, product evidence and a sense of a human operator at work;
- progressive, reversible scroll motion;
- hand-drawn marks when they point to one stable, meaningful piece of evidence;
- mobile interactions designed for hands, short attention and a changing viewport.

Krish rejects:

- generic grotesk plus fashionable serif combinations that make the site feel vibe-coded;
- decorative animation added only to make the page move;
- static boxes, weak diagrams and arbitrary dots;
- huge headings that wrap into awkward word stacks;
- fake labels, cryptic microcopy and performative CTAs;
- empty space that looks like a broken component;
- duplicate proof sections;
- screenshots treated as permanent art when the product will change;
- arrows or carousel controls that move when quote length changes;
- any public eyebrow pattern except the one frozen gateway label.
