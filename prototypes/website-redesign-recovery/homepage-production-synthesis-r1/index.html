You are not the first person to wonder what a new tool might take from you.

We have been asking that question for centuries.

370 BCWriting

If knowledge lives outside us, will memory grow weaker?

What changedIdeas could travel beyond one voice and survive their maker. We changed what memory was for.

1675The engine loom

If the machine can do the work, what happens to the worker?

What changedThe fear was not foolish. Jobs, wages and status changed. The real fight was over who controlled the gain.

1970sThe calculator

If the device does the arithmetic, will children stop learning to think?

What changedA review of 79 studies found no collapse in basic skills. The question moved from doing every sum to understanding the answer.

2000sSatnav

If the device knows the route, will we lose our sense of direction?

What changedThat risk turned out to be real. A useful tool still asks us what we choose to keep practising.
