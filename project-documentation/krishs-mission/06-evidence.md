# Evidence: where the proof lives

Status: Working pack. The one evidence register is growth-mindmake `docs/EVIDENCE.md` (checked 17 November 2026; E1 to E12, each with its source and limit). Its rules: quote a figure only with its source in the same sentence; re-check after 30 days; nothing marked "verify first" until checked against its publisher; never claim that institutional brains have replaced leaders, that AI damages thinking, or that executives specifically fall for wrong AI advice.

## The rows the pitch leans on

- **E1** (Vaccaro, Almaatouq and Malone, Nature Human Behaviour, 2024): human and AI combinations gained where the human was better than the AI at the task and lost where the AI was better. Limit: losses are measured against the AI working alone; never write "worse than without AI".
- **E3** (Dell'Acqua et al., Harvard Business School working paper 24-013, 2023): on a task outside AI's capability, consultants using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to get it right. The lead figure for posts.
- **E5** (Lee et al., CHI 2025): higher confidence in AI goes with less critical thinking; higher self-confidence with more. Self-reported.
- **E6** (Capgemini Research Institute, January 2026): more than half of C-suite executives use AI to inform strategic decisions.
- **E8** (World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025): analytical thinking is the top core skill, essential for seven in ten employers.
- **E9** (PwC 2026 AI Jobs Barometer, UK): AI user roles grew 65.8% in 2025 against 21.6% for AI developer roles.
- **E10** (Indeed Hiring Lab, 28 September 2026): AI in 9.7% of UK postings at end-August 2026, from 4.9% a year earlier.
- **E11 and E12** are the direction of travel only, never stated as fact.

## What the evidence does not show

No study measures automation bias in senior executives or boards. Evidence that AI wears down thinking is thin. There is no measured displacement of leaders. Say these honestly when asked.
