Every wave of automation has prompted the same fear: that the skilled people become unnecessary. AI sharpens the fear because it produces work that looks expert. A strategy memo, a pricing analysis and a hiring plan all arrive in seconds, fluent and plausible.

Fluent and plausible is exactly the problem. The work looks finished before anyone has decided whether it is right.

## Competent output is becoming cheap

For most knowledge work, the first draft used to be the expensive part. It took time, and the time sat with skilled people. AI has pushed the cost of a competent first draft close to zero, and it will keep falling.

When something becomes cheap, value moves to whatever remains scarce. In this case that is the ability to look at ten competent options and know which one fits this business, this customer and this moment, and which one will fail for reasons nobody put in the brief.

## What judgement actually consists of

Judgement is often described as instinct, which makes it sound unteachable. In practice it is made of things that can be named.

Standards: the quality bar a person applies without being asked. Memory: what was tried before, what happened and why. Exceptions: the cases where the usual rule is wrong. Taste: the sense of what a particular customer will find credible, or insulting.

AI has read about all of these in general. It has none of them for your business unless somebody gives them to it.

## Why the experts gain the most

The people who benefit most from AI are the ones who can tell good output from bad quickly. A senior person with clear standards can review ten AI drafts in the time it used to take to write one, and pick the right one. A person without those standards gets ten plausible drafts and no way to choose.

So AI raises the value of expertise twice. It removes the slow parts of expert work, and it increases the volume of output that needs expert judgement to sort.

## The risk for businesses

The danger is that judgement stays in a few senior heads while everything around it speeds up. Those people become the bottleneck, then they leave, and the judgement leaves with them.

The businesses that do well in the next economy will write their judgement down: the standards, the decisions and the reasons. That record can be taught to new people, used by AI systems and corrected when it turns out to be wrong. It compounds. A leader's own AI brain stays theirs, and the standards they share with the team stay with the business.
