# User Outcomes

---

## Outcome framework

Success is that the buyer walks away with an artefact they can defend and act on, inside a finish line fixed before the engagement started. Mindmaker is paid for resolved decisions and rebuilt commercial architecture, not for activity, slides or time.

Both engagements have a defined artefact, a defined finish line, and no retainer attached.

**A note on the numbers below.** Anything not labelled as observed is a target, not a measurement. The previous version of this file carried revenue forecasts built on offers that no longer exist and adoption percentages presented without qualification. The forecasts are gone. Every remaining figure is labelled inline where it appears, so a reader cannot pick one up mid-document and mistake it for a result.

## Teardown outcomes

### Emotional arc

```
Day 1:   Relief      The decision is finally written down and out of their head
Day 3:   Discomfort  Claims they had been treating as fact come back low-confidence
Day 7:   Clarity     What is theirs to decide separates from what the world decides
Day 10:  Resolve     A position they can put in front of a board without rehearsing
```

The discomfort in the middle is deliberate and worth protecting. A Teardown that only confirms what someone already believed has not done anything for them.

### Immediate, at delivery

- One decision resolved, in writing, on a page that can be forwarded without editing.
- The decision mapped to its load-bearing claims, each with a reliability tier.
- Every consideration classed External, Only you, or Nobody yet.
- The four-model cross-examination with disagreements preserved rather than averaged.
- Three claims placed under a 90-day watch.
- A CTRL workspace with the decision map in it.

### 30 days on

- The decision has held, or the watch list flagged the thing that should reopen it.
- The buyer has stopped relitigating it in leadership meetings, which is usually the outcome they actually wanted.
- Where the answer was "do not spend more", they did not.

### The honest outcome nobody advertises

A meaningful share of Teardowns end with a recommendation not to buy anything else. That is the product working. It is also what makes the recommendation worth something when it points the other way.

## Handover outcomes

### Immediate, at exit (week six)

- A rebuilt commercial layer: positioning, pricing and the sales motion, with the reasoning written down rather than held in one person's head.
- The team running the new motion themselves, having driven it in week four and run it without Krish in week five.
- A context layer the client owns, in plain text and version control, model-agnostic and not rented from a platform.
- No dependency to unwind, because there is nothing ongoing to cancel.

### Week five, specifically

The load-bearing outcome. Krish does not attend. Either the system runs without him or it does not, and both answers are useful while there is still a week left.

### Day 90

A recheck, included. The question is narrow and worth asking plainly: is the team still running it, or did it decay quietly once attention moved? A handover that decays quietly is not a handover.

### 6 to 12 months on

Target, not a measurement: the commercial layer has been changed by the client at least once without help. That is the real test. A system nobody can modify is a system they are renting.

## Outcome categories

### 1. Decision quality
The buyer can state the decision, the paths considered, the trade-off on each and why they rejected the others. In writing. This is the one that generalises: it is the same outcome whether the engagement was ten days or six weeks.

### 2. Commercial impact
Observed, from real engagements, and never presented as typical:

- A $254K POC contracted with a major US publisher.
- 40% production-time reduction, 75% reduction in campaign setup time, 22% revenue lift on affected inventory, built by an in-house team in 45 days with no new headcount.
- A $250K budget defended, first production workflow live in 90 days.
- First two pilots signed inside a 30-day window.

These are what happened at specific companies. They are not a forecast and should never be presented as one.

### 3. Confidence
The buyer stops dreading the question. The archetype file explains why this matters more than it sounds: the fraud feeling is rational rather than neurotic, so the fix is a defensible position rather than reassurance.

### 4. Independence
The explicit goal is that they do not need Mindmaker again. Referrals come from the result rather than from the invoice, and an engagement that creates dependency has failed on its own terms even if it is renewed.

## Anti-outcomes

- **Not** "AI transformation". Instead: one specific decision, or a rebuilt commercial layer, finished.
- **Not** a 40-slide strategy deck. Instead: a one-page memo, or a working commercial layer the team runs.
- **Not** an ongoing retainer or a fractional role. Instead: fixed scope, fixed price, fixed finish line.
- **Not** tool recommendations. Instead: trade-off analysis, so the buyer decides.
- **Not** implementation or production IT. Instead: the commercial and decision layer, with a clean handover.
- **Not** a six-month engagement. Instead: ten business days, or six weeks.
- **Not** certainty. Instead: the disagreement preserved, so the buyer can see where the answer is soft.
