As AI takes on more of the work, most teams reach for a simple safety rule: a human in the loop, everywhere. In practice that rule decays quickly. The volume of AI output grows, the person in the loop starts approving by habit, and the review becomes a formality that nobody would defend if something went wrong.

A better approach is to decide clearly which decisions stay with people, and let AI carry the rest.

## Three tests for what stays human

Reach. If the work will be seen or relied on by someone outside the business, such as a customer, a partner or a regulator, a person releases it. The business is accountable for what it says to the world, and accountability needs a name attached.

Reversibility. If a mistake can be undone cheaply, delegation is safe. If it cannot, such as a signed contract, a published price or a dismissal, a person decides.

Standard-setting. Some decisions define what good looks like for everything that follows: the pricing principle, the brand voice, the hiring bar. These are judgement calls about direction, and they belong to the people who will answer for the direction.

## What can be delegated

Most work falls outside those three tests. Internal drafts, research, analysis, preparation, first versions and summaries are reversible and stay inside the business. AI can do this work at speed, with people sampling the results and correcting patterns rather than approving each item.

This is where most of the time savings are. The effort moves to AI; the accountability stays with people.

## Let the line move, and record why

The line is a working rule, and it should change as evidence arrives. A team might start with every customer email released by a person, then find that a certain kind of reply is always right, and delegate it.

The important thing is to record each change and its reason. Without that record the line drifts quietly, and six months later nobody knows why AI is now sending something that used to need a signature. With it, the team can see how its own rules evolved and correct them if the evidence changes.

## The result

Drawn this way, the line gives people fewer things to approve and more reason to approve them carefully. That is what oversight was supposed to mean in the first place.
