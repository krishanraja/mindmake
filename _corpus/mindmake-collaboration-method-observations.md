# Mindmake collaboration method observations

Captured: 23 August 2026.

Status: internal working evidence only. This is not a public offer, a finished method or permission to train a model on client material.

## Why this exists

The Mindmake rebuild took place through a long, high-attention collaboration rather than one prompt and one answer. Krish asked how the quality of that process might be made usable by a customer who is less inquisitive, technical or visually confident.

This note records what can be observed from the work. It does not pretend that one session proves a universal method.

## What produced the quality

### 1. The work kept changing level

The conversation moved between business model, buyer, offer, proof, language, visual system, interaction, data, lead capture and delivery economics. A correction at one level was allowed to change another. The website was not treated as a skin over an unchanged offer.

### 2. Reactions became usable evidence

Krish did not only say that a version was wrong. He named the felt failure:

- generic rather than ownable;
- technically impressive but too much like ChatGPT;
- visually empty before an animation began;
- too static or too much like a normal vertical build;
- proof shown as a table when it should feel consequential;
- copy that preached rather than gave value;
- large type breaking into accidental fragments;
- an AI demonstration making the business look replaceable by AI;
- a lead path that devalued the work by opening the diary to anyone.

Those reactions were converted into tests, frozen negative evidence and design rules. They were not smoothed into vague preferences.

### 3. A better version had to beat the previous one

Each material area was compared with the strongest previous state. A new version was not accepted merely because it was cleaner or newer. It had to retain good proof, motion, character and useful features while reducing confusion.

### 4. Simple on top did not mean simple underneath

The public surface kept moving towards two clear doors and one guided next step. Underneath, the business and product model became richer: company research, human judgment, returned-time value, a 30-day proof, continuation economics, consent, evidence rules and a private operator digest.

### 5. Blank questionnaires were rejected

The strongest interaction begins by doing useful work from one small input. It reflects the company back, shows what it can and cannot establish, then asks a choice that changes the result. The user is not asked to describe everything the system could have researched.

### 6. Negative evidence was preserved

Rejected prototypes were kept with the reason for rejection. This stopped later work from repeating familiar failures, including generic component language, empty initial states, weak abstract animation and overcooked AI theatre.

### 7. Approval was separated from execution

Material decisions were locked before production work. Reversible local implementation continued autonomously inside that lock. Deletion, live data, email sending, deployment and production promotion stayed separate authority gates.

### 8. Verification shaped the design

The work was checked across routes, widths, keyboard states, reduced motion, long testimonials, headings, crawler output and failure states. Mobile was treated as its own composition problem, not a desktop page squeezed into one column.

## What a future customer may not bring

A less practised customer may struggle to:

- name why an output feels wrong;
- separate a surface problem from a business-model problem;
- remember an earlier version that solved one part better;
- see which correction should become a permanent rule;
- supply useful examples without being asked concrete questions;
- stay with the work through several uncomfortable revisions;
- judge whether AI is helping them think or merely producing more material.

The method therefore cannot depend on a customer behaving exactly like Krish. It must create the missing structure for them.

## A possible bottled process

This is a hypothesis to test, not a product lock.

1. **Read before asking.** Gather the company, role, existing work, public signals and supplied files.
2. **Return a useful mirror.** Show a first view with sources, limits and one real decision beneath the request.
3. **Offer real alternatives.** Present two or three materially different paths, not cosmetic variants.
4. **Extract the reaction.** Ask what feels wrong, what became clearer and what must survive. Use concrete choices when the customer cannot name the reason.
5. **Turn reactions into rules.** Record the accepted decision, rejected pattern, evidence, confidence and where the rule applies.
6. **Freeze a floor.** Define the strongest approved version and the tests a later version must still pass.
7. **Build autonomously inside the lock.** Bring the customer back only for a choice that would materially change the outcome, risk or authority.
8. **Verify the whole journey.** Test the real artefact, edge cases and failure states rather than only reviewing screenshots.
9. **Hand back the memory.** Leave the customer with the decisions, reasons, examples, tests and working system so quality can continue without the original conversation.

## The unit of memory

A reusable decision record may need:

- the observed problem;
- the user's original words;
- the relevant evidence or artefact;
- options considered;
- the chosen direction;
- rejected patterns and why they failed;
- the scope of the rule;
- confidence and what would change it;
- a regression example;
- the next authority gate;
- the result after real use.

This is more useful than saving a transcript alone. The transcript is evidence. The decision record is the working memory.

## Product principles to test

- Give value before asking for effort.
- Ask only questions that change the work or the hand-off.
- Use the customer's language, then make it clearer without flattening it.
- Make sources and uncertainty visible.
- Show a worked state before an empty state.
- Let a user react to something concrete instead of inventing the answer from scratch.
- Preserve human taste and judgment as explicit inputs, not noise to automate away.
- Keep the interface simple while the evidence, memory and checks deepen underneath.
- Do not turn patience into needless waiting. Long collaboration is valuable only when each pass improves the artefact or the rules.
- Do not create dependence. The final system should help the customer keep improving the work.

## Early pass signals

A future version is promising if a less technical user can:

- reach a sharper problem than the one they first described;
- explain why the recommendation fits their business;
- correct the system without learning prompt craft;
- see that a correction changes later work;
- compare the new output with the approved floor;
- leave with a useful artefact and a reusable decision memory.

It fails if it becomes a long chatbot interview, hides weak evidence behind confident prose, asks questions whose answers do not change anything, or produces more polished material without improving the decision.

## New observation: move from the instance to the governing rule

The eyebrow correction exposed a repeatable failure in AI collaboration. Krish rejected a visible pattern, but local corrections removed only the examples currently named. The same device returned as a kicker, chapter number, status strap, proof badge or diagram label.

The useful signal was not a dislike of one line of copy. It was a rejection of a whole visual function: decorative preamble that asks the reader to process a small label before the real point. It felt templated, diluted the hierarchy and made the work look AI-generated.

This suggests three levels of feedback:

1. **Instance:** this element is wrong here.
2. **Pattern family:** every element doing the same job is wrong across this artifact.
3. **Doctrine:** the person rejects this design move across future work unless they explicitly reopen it.

The AI must identify when feedback has moved up a level. Repeated local editing is not responsiveness if the shared mechanism survives elsewhere.

For a material correction, the process should now:

1. preserve the user's exact objection;
2. name the underlying function, not only the visible class or words;
3. search the full artifact for synonyms, variants and dormant states;
4. state the narrow exceptions required for function or access;
5. write the rule into the current canon and decision history;
6. freeze the approved artifact with a stable hash;
7. add a source and rendered regression check;
8. compare every later version with that floor before asking for approval.

This is an example of bottling Krish's judgment without reducing it to a transcript. The durable memory is the reason, scope, exception and test.
