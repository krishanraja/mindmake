## 2. Build Your AI Brain

**The proposition, copy-grade.** For a leader who handed the AI thing to someone else and is losing the understanding a leader now needs. Over thirty days, you and Krish build a working system that holds how you judge, choose and create, and uses it on your real work. You are not buying an agency's output or a coach's questions. You are buying capability that stays: a brain that is yours, that compounds after the work ends, and that never required you to become technical to own it.

**Buyer and budget.** The coaching and chief of staff line. Senior leaders already spend at this altitude for this exact person; the contrast is that their notes leave with them and this system stays. (Internal budget label only; neither phrase appears in public copy.)

**The moment.** I handed AI to someone else and I am losing the understanding. Second beat on the page: my taste and standards are trapped in my head.

**The unit of value.** Capability kept. Time returned is the setup, never the promise.

**The mechanism, copy-grade.** The engine is the Mindmake brain: CTRL plus a corpus built over years from behavioural psychology, AI agent harnesses, and real work and corrections with leaders across keynotes, cohorts and advisory. The craft in front of it is Krish's extraction: the questions, comparisons and graded examples that pull out taste a leader cannot name unaided. The rule above both: the system never does the work for them. It accelerates them building their own brain. A generic AI has no memory of you and no method to extract you. A consultancy extracts you into a deck that leaves. An LLM alone cannot hold your standards, sources and corrections as a compounding system. The Mindmake brain, with Krish beside you, is the only version where the understanding stays yours and keeps growing after he leaves.

**Claim control.** The figure of more than 1,000 leaders is Krish's stated basis and is strong. It does not render publicly until the evidence trail is compiled and approved (candidate trail: 30+ keynotes, 1,600+ Lightning Lesson signups, cohort and advisory corrections). Until then, public copy says leaders, without a count.

**The nots.** Not an agency. Not a coach. (Pair locked; on-page order is a mock-stage decision.)

**The ladder.** One numbered section per step.

1. **Capture.** We sit inside your real work. Krish's extraction plus the Mindmake brain pull out the taste, judgement, standards and trusted context that make you good. You start from a working engine, never a blank page.
2. **Encode.** That thinking becomes a working system in CTRL: memory, sources, rules and checks. Not a folder of notes.
3. **Amplify.** Your strengths arrive early. The system prepares work your way and brings what matters before you ask.
4. **Uncover.** It shows you what you are not seeing: the calls you avoid, the patterns you miss, the blind spots nobody tells you about.
5. **Keep.** The system, the evidence and the reasons stay with you. It compounds without Krish.

**Clarity mandate.** This is a new category, so the page defines before it sells. One plain definition sits high on the page, copy-grade: an AI brain is a working system that holds your taste, judgement, standards, memory and trusted context, and uses them on your real work. Every section passes the ten-year-old test. The page must feel like access and acceleration, never like software doing the leader's job.

## 3. Build Your AI GTM

**The proposition, copy-grade.** Two leaders arrive here. The established leader, whose customers can now do part of it alone and whose price and promise no longer line up. And the builder, creating a new AI-native digital business, whose model has to be invented rather than repaired. Krish has spent fifteen years turning data and AI into customer experience and monetisation strategies. Both leaders get the same month: read what changed, choose the lever, build the model, prove it with buyers, run it. One important call made defensible, and a model the team runs without him.

**Buyer and budget.** The positioning and pricing project line, where workshop-format engagements are already reported at $50,000 to $100,000+. Mindmake is the version that builds the model into a working system and proves it with real buyers.

**The two moments.** Lead, established: customers can now do part of this alone, and the price and promise no longer line up. Co-lead, builder: you are inventing the model, not repairing it, and speed without a model is just burn. One page, two entry moments, the same five steps. The mock must prove both moments read clean without mushing; if they mush, the fallback is two hero states over one shared ladder, never two pages.

**The unit of value.** Decision quality. One product, price, positioning or people call moves the P&L by far more than the fee. For the builder, choosing the model is itself the decision. Name the decision in the brief and the fee prices itself.

**The nots.** Not a fractional hire. Not an automation shop. (Krish's pick. The removed refusal's substance survives inside step 3: the model has to run, not read well. Flip rule in section 7.)

**The ladder.** One numbered section per step.

1. **Read.** We find what changed: what customers can now do alone, what they will still pay for, where your numbers disagree with your story. For the builder: what buyers will pay for that did not exist a year ago.
2. **Choose the lever.** Product, price, positioning or people. One lever carries the month; the model covers all four. One lever deep, the whole model mapped.
3. **Build the model.** An AI-native GTM model you can interrogate, with sources attached. It has to run, not read well.
4. **Prove with buyers.** The model meets real buyers before the month ends. Evidence beats opinion.
5. **Run it.** The model, the data and the reasons stay with your team. The next call starts sharper.

## 4. One practice

- **Mechanism lead, both doors:** working understanding. You can hand over the work. Not the understanding. CTRL proves it on screen. Operator history floors it. The method is described, never named, until it survives three clients who are nothing like Krish.
- **Month shape:** one brick, all the way. One capability or one lever taken to live use inside 24 to 30 hours of Krish's time, the read folded into week one. Refusing the second brick mid-month is part of the offer.
- **Doors:** two doors public, directional routing private. Principals tend to enter through Brain and buy GTM next; companies enter through GTM and the leader buys Brain next. The routing lives in brief review and follow-ups, nowhere public.
- **Continuation, visible and earned:** the pages show how the work compounds past day thirty as an interactive timeline. Day 30 is the proof and the only thing a visitor can start; days 60 and 90 show what continuation typically builds, labelled as earned, with no prices, package names or durations for sale. Privately, a continuation is proposed only against named deliverables discovered in month one, never as generic retained time. Duration follows the deliverable; the deliverable follows the proof.
- **Register:** confident on the page, category on stage.
- **Section look:** the full-bleed background numeral is approved as the step mark, conditional on the numbered sections remaining true sequences. The canon amendment is in section 8.

## 5. The pricing strategy, locked

No public price. The internal rate card is exact.

| Altitude | 30-day proof | Three months | Effective rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Principal | $15,000 | $36,000, proof credited as month one | $500 to $625 an hour |
| Company | $25,000 | $60,000, proof credited as month one | $833 to $1,040 an hour |

**The altitude rule.** The card follows whose P&L the decision moves. If the model or capability decision moves a trading company's P&L, the company card, including funded AI-native builders. A solo principal, or a pre-revenue builder deciding their own model, takes the principal card. Ability to pay never argues the craft down; it argues the stakes up.

**Constraint checks.** Both cards satisfy the one-third rule: $15,000 is more than a third of $36,000 and $25,000 is more than a third of $60,000. Both clear the private floor of well above $300 an hour. At full utilisation (two proofs plus one continuation) revenue runs roughly $45,000 to $65,000 a month, a personal ceiling near $550,000 to $700,000 a year. Volume is capped by design, so price is the only lever on that ceiling.

**Floor and flex.** $12,000 cash floor. Below it only when the gap is paid in a named asset written into the agreement: equity, participation, real distribution, or a consented case study. Exposure is not payment.

**Public stance and the script.** No number anywhere public. When asked: the proof is a five figure month, and the exact number follows the brief.

**Collapse target.** After five paid proofs with door-shaped stories, collapse upward toward a single higher number.

**Continuation deals.** A continuation proposal names its deliverables, discovered in month one. The three-month totals above are the rate frame those proposals price inside; longer or larger scopes price off the same effective rates.
