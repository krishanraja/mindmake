# Ideal Customer Profile

**One ICP.** This used to describe three audiences with no overlap plus a retention layer, which made sense when there were six rungs aimed at different buyers. Only one of those audiences has a live surface now, so the rest went with the offers.

> **Deep archetype:** the full psychographic, behavioural and skill-gap profile of this buyer, "The Accountable Delegator", lives in `ICP_ACCOUNTABLE_DELEGATOR.md`. It is the best-written document in this estate and it is still accurate. This file is the operational summary.

---

## The ICP

**Companies of 50 to 5,000 people, sweet spot 100 to 1,000. The buyer is the CEO, CRO or VP Product: the seat accountable for whether it sells.**

Not the CTO. This is commercial work, and engineering and commercial are different problems. The most common misroute is a technically-curious CTO who finds the method interesting but cannot act on the output, because the decision it produces is not theirs to make.

### Firmographic context

| Attribute | Range |
|---|---|
| Employees | 50 to 5,000, sweet spot 100 to 1,000 |
| Stage | Series B to public, or mid-market privately held |
| Revenue | $10M to $500M, sweet spot $25M to $250M |
| Budget authority | Enough to sign for the relevant decision without a committee |
| Accountability | Owns whether the thing sells, not whether it ships |

**Geography is deliberately not a qualifier.** The site sells internationally, which is why it carries three currencies. No document, meta tag or structured-data field should state or imply a market.

### Psychographic signals

These are the durable part of this document and they have not changed:

- Has been pitched fourteen AI tools this quarter and committed to none.
- Says some version of "I should understand this but I don't."
- Presents AI strategies to boards they cannot actually execute.
- Watches AI-native competitors moving faster and feels the cost of catching up compounding.
- Trusts peers more than vendors.
- Can describe the decision in one sentence when pushed, and has been avoiding writing it down.

### How they talk about the problem

- "I need to deliver an AI strategy and I don't know where to start."
- "Fourteen tools pitched this quarter. I use none of them."
- "Everyone on my team is using something different. It's chaos."
- "Should we build our own or buy off the shelf?"
- "How do I know if this is delivering anything or just hype?"
- "I'm nervous about getting locked into the wrong vendor."

## Which rung

Two questions decide it, in this order.

**How formed is the decision?**

- A nameable decision, unresolved, with a real cost of getting it wrong: **The Teardown.** This is the default and the honest first spend for almost everyone.
- The decision is already made, and what is broken is how the business decides and sells: **The Handover.** Still starts with a Teardown.
- No nameable decision at all: **neither.** A free on-ramp, and come back when a real fork shows up. Do not sell a Teardown to someone with nothing to tear down.

**How big is the company?** Only relevant for the Handover, where it sets the price band: under 100, 100 to 250, or 250 to 5,000.

---

## The third door

Funds, family offices and operating partners are a **door, not a separate ICP.** They buy the same two engagements on behalf of a portfolio company that fits the profile above. What changes is who is holding the invoice and that they are watching a pattern across a portfolio rather than one company's quarter.

---

## Disqualifiers

- Cannot name a specific decision in a sentence.
- Wants a fractional executive seat or ongoing capacity.
- Wants a retainer.
- Wants implementation or production IT.
- Wants training or a certification. Mindmaker does not sell training.
- Pre-revenue, or pre-product-market-fit.
- An individual contributor with no budget.
- Above 5,000 people, where the published bands stop applying and the shape is a different conversation.

The right response to a disqualifier is to say so warmly and name an alternative, including referring someone else for implementation work. A walk-away that is handled well is worth more than an engagement that stalls.

---

## Anti-ICP

The buyer who wants faster outputs and will not reinvest the time saved. They light up at speed and go flat at owning context. The honest recommendation there is a tool, and to keep their money.

Krish's line for it: *"Not everyone needs a fleet, and I'll tell you that to your face even though I sell the fleet. Buy a tool, get your faster outputs, stop there."*
