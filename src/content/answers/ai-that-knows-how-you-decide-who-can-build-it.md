Every AI tool a leader can buy has read the internet. It knows the market, the jargon and the average answer. It knows nothing about the leader using it: which deals they walk away from, which numbers they distrust, which people they call first. So the leader gets faster at drafting and no better at deciding.

That gap is what people mean when they ask for an AI that knows them. It is a fair thing to want, and a hard thing to buy off a shelf.

## What it has to hold

An AI that knows how you decide needs more than your files. It needs five things, kept in a form it can use on the next piece of work:

- **Your taste.** What good looks like to you, in writing, in a deal, in a hire.
- **Your standards.** The lines you will not cross and the checks you always run.
- **Your judgement.** How you made past calls and why, including the ones you got wrong.
- **Your memory.** The people, the history and the context you carry in your head.
- **Your trusted sources.** Where you look first, and which sources you have learned to ignore.

Most of this already exists in emails, board papers, notes and past decisions. The work is drawing it out, checking it with you, and building it into the tools you already use.

## How to judge who can build it

Plenty of people will offer to build you a custom AI. Five questions separate a working system from a demonstration:

1. **Does it run on your real work?** A system tested on examples you made up for the purpose will fail on the first real decision.
2. **Is it yours?** It should be private to you, in your own accounts, and go with you if you move on.
3. **Can you correct it in plain words?** If every correction needs the builder, you have hired a dependency.
4. **Does it get sharper as you use it?** Each correction should make the next answer better, which means corrections have to be kept.
5. **Will the builder tell you where it breaks?** Honest builders show you the limits before you find them.
